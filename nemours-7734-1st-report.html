Section Three

Why some inquiries go quiet

The numbers above are raw counts, every inquiry that came in with nothing filtered out. We report them that way on purpose, and it means some of those 12 will never produce feedback for us to pass along.

Becky follows up personally with every inquiry, by phone and by text, and every prospect is pointed to our Resident Selection Criteria before a showing is arranged. That is where some of them stop. Once a prospect sees they would not qualify, because of credit, income, a pet, or a move-in date that does not work, they usually stop replying rather than tell us so.

What that does and does not mean

It does not mean the property fell short. In almost every case the prospect never raised an objection about the property, the photos or the rent. The conversation ended over qualification.

It does not mean we stopped following up. Every inquiry is called, texted and invited to tour, and we follow up again before treating a lead as closed.

It does mean raw inquiry counts overstate the qualified pool. Twelve inquiries is not twelve possible residents. What matters is how many are qualified, available on your timeline and still interested after a real conversation.

We would far rather a prospect step away at that stage than submit an application we have to decline, and far rather either of those than place a resident who should not have been approved.

Section Four

Our read on pricing

Four days is early, and we hold any pricing opinion loosely at this stage. But the evidence so far points one way.

  • Inquiries are coming in steadily, with Zillow producing most of them, which tells us the listing is being seen by the right renters.
  • Inquiries are turning into showings. Four tours in four days means renters like what they see enough to visit.
  • The feedback is positive, with one couple very interested and no objection raised about the rent.

When a price is too high, the pattern shows up quickly: inquiries dry up in the first week, few of them turn into showings, and the prospects who do tour raise the rent as an objection. We are seeing none of those.

Our recommendation is to hold at the current price. There is no decision we are asking you to make this week.
